# Study-planner and auto-scheduling apps: features Cruscotto Studio lacks

Scope: student planners (Shovel, MyStudyLife, Notion templates, TickTick, Structured, Sunsama, YPT) and auto-schedulers (Motion, Reclaim.ai, Akiflow, Morgen, Trevor), compared with Cruscotto Studio's current features: Calendar-read sessions, Done/Partial/Not done/Skipped/Undo, Imprevisto, subject weights and quotas, deterministic 7-10 day ricalibro with preview, exercises, Leitner flashcards, Notion summary.
Research date: 2026-11-19. Most comparison pages are vendor or competitor blogs (Morgen, DormWay, Coursicle, Saner, Lifestack, ClickUp); this is flagged where it matters. I could not reach Reddit threads directly. Reddit sentiment below comes from secondhand summaries.

## 1. What student planners offer for planning, progress tracking and motivation

### Takeaway
The most valued planning feature in this category is a **capacity check**: it compares estimated work with real free hours and says you are over-committed, or ahead or behind (Shovel's "Cushion", Sunsama's workload warning). Next come **guided daily and weekly rituals** (plan in the morning, shut down in the evening, carry over what is unfinished, set weekly objectives), **exam countdowns with urgency**, **grade tracking**, and **focus statistics**. Cruscotto Studio already has weights, quotas and a ricalibro, but it has no explicit "can I fit this before the exam?" verdict, no daily or weekly ritual screen, and no history or statistics view.

### Cited Findings
**Shovel (paid study planner, about $35/yr or $9.79/mo, 7-day trial)**
- "The Cushion" (Time Cushion) estimates how long each task takes, subtracts it from your real free hours, shows whether you are ahead or behind, and warns when the workload exceeds your free time. — [DormWay vs Shovel (competitor blog)](https://dormway.app/blog/dormway-vs-shovel)
- It plans work against available hours rather than due dates alone. It handles rotating and block schedules, tracks weighted grades, and imports a syllabus PDF with AI, with a review screen before tasks are created. LMS sync (Canvas, Moodle, Brightspace, Google Classroom) is read-only, about every 24 h. — [DormWay vs Shovel](https://dormway.app/blog/dormway-vs-shovel)
- Coursicle, a competitor, says Shovel calculates whether you have enough available study hours to finish what is due, and that no free app in this category does this properly. This is marketing-influenced. — [Coursicle blog](https://www.coursicle.com/blog/shovel-vs-coursicle/)
- Tasks can be timeboxed to a study time by drag and drop. Reviews mention a daily timeline, repeating schedules and a built-in timer. — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/products/shovel/); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1467742357)
- Reception is mixed: about 4.0 to 4.1 stars on iOS (about 190 to 222 ratings) and 3.4 on Android (30 reviews). Complaints cover bugs ("The iOS app has tons of bugs. Web app is also slow"), price ("What student can afford that?"), and the fact that the mobile app is only a companion to the web platform. — [appfollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/shovel-study-planner/1467742357?country=us); [appwisp Android](https://appwisp.com/app/android/com.howtostudyincollege.shovel); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1467742357)

**MyStudyLife (free, "10M+ students")**
- It has tasks for assignments, revision sessions and reminders, and several study tasks per exam. It also offers an exam countdown, revision planning and exam reminders as the date approaches, grade tracking and personal stats, plus an AI assistant ("Scout") that builds a revision plan for a specific exam. These claims come from the vendor's own pages. — [MyStudyLife care article](https://care.mystudylife.com/en/articles/9502621-how-to-track-assignments-revision-and-reminders-in-mystudylife); [mystudylife.com](https://mystudylife.com/)
- Students, teachers and lecturers praise it as free and ad-free. — [StudentBeans](https://www.studentbeans.com/blog/uk/10-best-study-planner-apps-for-students)

**Notion student templates (community-made, many free)**
- They offer a running grade or GPA per class from grades entered, an exam countdown with remaining days and colour-coded urgency (Finals Atlas), an assessment tracker with days left, mark weighting, score and grade calculator, and a study-sessions tracker. — [bookscouter](https://bookscouter.com/blog/best-notion-templates-for-students/); [Finals Atlas](https://notionhub-6jk.pages.dev/en/templates/finals-atlas-exam-countdown/); [Notion Everything Student Dashboard](https://notioneverything.com/templates/notion-student-dashboard)

**Sunsama ($20 to 22/mo, no free plan)**
- The morning ritual pulls in tasks, asks for a time estimate on each, checks the total against available hours, and lets you drag tasks onto the calendar. The reviewer called it "a daily coaching session that takes 5 minutes." — [Asian Efficiency review](https://www.asianefficiency.com/schedule-management/sunsama-review/)
- The workload warning example is "You're over-committed by 4 hours. What can you move to tomorrow?" The reviewer calls it "the killer feature". — [Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/schedule-management/sunsama-review/)
- The daily shutdown reviews what was done, carries forward unfinished work ("Everything I didn't finish today is already scheduled for tomorrow"), offers an optional reflection prompt, and makes a "clean break". — [Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/schedule-management/sunsama-review/)
- Weekly planning sets 3 to 5 focus objectives. Other reviews describe a weekly review that checks progress against the objectives with a short journal, plus a weekly workload chart based on actual time. — [Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/schedule-management/sunsama-review/); [dupple](https://dupple.com/tools/sunsama)
- Reviewers present the calm design, with "no gamification, streaks, or badges", as a strength. — [Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/schedule-management/sunsama-review/)

**Structured (daily timeline planner)**
- An Energy Monitor (since v3.4, Feb 2024; Android May 2025) lets you give tasks an energy level and shows daily energy use on the timeline. The monthly view can show each day's scheduled energy level. — [Structured changelog](https://feedback.structured.app/changelog/stuctured-34-energy-monitor); [Structured 4.0 help](https://help.structured.app/en/articles/3232066)
- Overdue inbox tasks are dragged onto the timeline. "Replan" (rescheduling of overdue tasks) is a Pro feature. Cool Curation rates the app 4/5 and warns of feature creep. — [Cool Curation review](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk)

**TickTick**
- Focus statistics show time per task, a daily focus record, weekly and monthly trends, and your most productive hours. You can set an estimated pomo count per task and compare it with actual sessions; TickTick says the gap teaches better estimation. Habit tracking with streaks and frequency goals is a separate feature. — [TickTick blog](https://blog.ticktick.com/2021/01/11/the-pomodoro-technique/); [ClickUp review of TickTick](https://clickup.com/learn/topic/task-management/tools/ticktick/)

**YPT / Yeolpumta (free + IAP, about 5.6M Google Play installs)**
- It is a subject-based study timer that shows study time per subject and shares it with group or friends for ranking. Users praise "compete with friends, see how many hours they studied". Google Play lists data collection in six categories. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yeolpumta/id1441909643); [appgoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.pallo.passiontimerscoped)

### Inferences
- Capacity check ("Cushion" or workload warning): Cruscotto has recommended vs planned hours per subject, but no single verdict like "you need 38 h for Analisi before the exam, you have 31 h of free study slots, so you are 7 h behind". All the data is local (Calendar free slots, weights, estimates), so this fits the no-backend PWA. It is the most praised feature across both product families.
- Morning plan and evening shutdown ritual: a guided "Oggi" opening (confirm the day's sessions, see the over-commitment) and closing (mark what was left unmarked, carry it over, write a one-line note). This could replace or feed the "copy summary for Notion". It is local only.
- Weekly review: hours done vs planned per subject, Leitner backlog, exercises solved, 3 objectives for next week. It is local only.
- Exam countdown with urgency colour on the home screen. The app already uses exam urgency in its weights, so this is mostly UI.
- Grade or "voto" tracking per exam, and a weighted average for Italian or Chinese credits (CFU or credit-weighted GPA). It is local only. It is lower priority because the app is about study time, not grades.
- Per-subject history and stats (hours per subject per week, % of sessions done, partial rate, best time of day). It is local only and computed from Dexie.
- Social ranking (YPT) needs a server. Skip it.
- Syllabus or LMS import needs an LLM or a server. The optional AI path could parse a syllabus into topics, but it is not core.

### Gaps
- I found no independent user data on how much students actually use Sunsama's or Shovel's capacity warnings. The praise comes from reviewers and competitors.
- I did not verify current MyStudyLife or TickTick paywall boundaries.

## 2. What auto-schedulers do beyond "move to first free slot"

### Takeaway
Reclaim and Motion add **chunking** (minimum and maximum block length, spreading across days), **deadline-aware ordering with hard and soft deadlines**, **"at risk" or "past deadline" issue lists**, **habits with ideal time, flexible windows and min/max duration that get more protected as the calendar fills**, **buffers or breaks between blocks**, a **"schedule after" date**, and **priority-based dropping of low-priority habits when the week is full**. Morgen and Akiflow use a semi-manual model, which matches Cruscotto's confirm-first rule: the AI or rules propose and the user approves ("Replan undone tasks", Frames for work types or energy).

### Cited Findings
**Reclaim.ai**
- Tasks: priority P1 to P4, due date, duration in 15-min increments, scheduling hours, a "Schedule after" date, and min and max session length. "Split up" spreads the task over multiple sessions before the due date. Nearer deadlines are placed first. "The closer you get to the deadline, the more aggressively the Task is defended with busy blocks." "Up Next" ranks tasks manually. — [Reclaim blog: Habits vs Tasks](https://reclaim.ai/blog/habits-vs-tasks)
- Example from older docs: a 6 h task with 1 h minimum and 2 h maximum blocks is scheduled as 2 + 1 + 1.5 h... Reclaim "will use the max duration to 'space out' your Tasks" so blocks don't stack back to back. — [Reclaim help: key concepts](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/4303610-key-concepts-in-reclaim-tasks); [default task settings](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/5153635-default-task-settings)
- If you delete a task event, Reclaim does not reduce the remaining duration. It reschedules that time later, so "Calendar is the source of truth" for done time. — [Reclaim help](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/4303610-key-concepts-in-reclaim-tasks)
- Reclaim 2.0 has an "At Risk" recommendation category for tasks that "may need attention because of their timing, priority, or due date". Task events flip between Free and Busy depending on how at-risk they are. — [Reclaim 2.0 tasks overview](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/16558552-reclaim-2-0-tasks-overview); [Reclaim help](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/4303610-key-concepts-in-reclaim-tasks)
- Habits have a frequency, a time window (e.g. 8-16 weekdays), an "ideal time", and a min/max duration (a lunch can shrink from 60 to 30 on busy days). Their priority is P1 to P4, and lower-priority habits are skipped for the week when time is short. Defense levels are "Most flexible / Let Reclaim decide / Most defensive". A habit starts as tentative "free" and switches to "busy" as rescheduling options run out. — [Reclaim blog: Habits vs Tasks](https://reclaim.ai/blog/habits-vs-tasks)
- Reclaim also adds automatic buffer breaks and travel time, and Focus Time is free. Complaints: duplicated events across calendars, and scheduling links treating task blocks as busy. — [Morgen: Motion vs Reclaim (vendor blog)](https://morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-vs-reclaim)

**Motion**
- It schedules by priority, duration and deadline, and the daily plan is based on capacity. It "Continuously reshuffles your day/week when conflicts arise", and its "Set schedule" sets preferred windows (e.g. a morning block and an afternoon block). — [Morgen: Motion vs Reclaim](https://morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-vs-reclaim)
- A "Resolve Issues" box marks in red the hard-deadline tasks that can't fit or are scheduled past the deadline, and in orange the soft-deadline ones. Past-due tasks get a red "!". Fixes offered: extend the deadline, shorten the duration or chunk size, or turn on hard deadline. A hard deadline may schedule outside work hours; a soft one won't. — [Motion help: Resolve Issues box](https://help.usemotion.com/product-and-feature-guides/calendar/tasks/understanding-the-resolve-issues-box); [Motion help: tasks not scheduling](https://help.usemotion.com/tasks-events-and-auto-scheduling-101/tasks-not-scheduling-where-you-expect-heres-whats-actually-happening)
- Chunking: the minimum chunk is 15 min, and the typical maximum is half the task duration. Tasks that can't fit stay unscheduled with a warning until you change their parameters. — [Motion help: chunked tasks](https://help.usemotion.com/tasks-events-and-auto-scheduling-101/chunked-tasks-how-and-when-to-use-them)
- ClickUp users requested an "auto scheduled task is ngmi (not going to make it)" warning, which shows the at-risk signal is wanted and not universal. — [ClickUp feedback](https://feedback.clickup.com/feature-requests/p/auto-scheduled-task-is-ngmi-not-going-to-make-it)

**Morgen, Akiflow, Trevor**
- Morgen AI Planner uses "Frames", recurring blocks per kind of work (morning deep work, afternoon admin). It suggests tasks into matching frames, and you approve the schedule before it goes live. Morgen says it weighs priority, due date, estimates and energy. This is a vendor claim. — [Morgen: what is an AI planner](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/ai-planner); [Morgen: best AI planning assistants](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/best-ai-planning-assistants)
- Akiflow uses manual time-blocking plus "Replan Undone Tasks", which suggests the next available slots. Lifestack, a competitor, says it has no energy awareness. — [Morgen: Akiflow vs Motion](https://morgen.so/blog-posts/akiflow-vs-motion); [Lifestack](https://lifestack.ai/blog/morgen-alternative)
- Trevor is a cheap (about $5/mo) drag-to-calendar time-blocker with a daily planning ritual. — [Morgen roundup](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/best-ai-planning-assistants)

### Inferences
These are things Cruscotto lacks. All of them can be built in the deterministic planner with local data:
- At-risk list per subject or exam: sessions needed before the exam vs free slots left, with red and orange like Motion. Remedies to offer: lower the target, add slots, or drop low-weight subjects.
- Min/max block length and spacing when the ricalibro or the "move" creates or splits sessions (e.g. never under 30 min, never two blocks of the same subject back to back, at most 2 h per block).
- Buffers: leave N minutes between a lesson and a study block, or between study blocks, when moving to the first free slot.
- Preferred windows or energy per session type (e.g. "Study" new material in the morning, "Review" and flashcards in the evening, "Exercises" in the afternoon). This is the Structured Energy Monitor and Morgen Frames idea, as a simple setting.
- "Schedule after" or earliest date for remainder sessions and reviews (Review +1w shouldn't be moved earlier than +1w).
- Priority-based dropping: when the week overflows, propose to drop or postpone the lowest-weight review sessions first (Reclaim's skip low-priority habits), shown in the preview.
- Protected recurring blocks such as meals, sleep and rest days, which the ricalibro never fills. This is the Reclaim habit defense, without Calendar writes.
- "Replan undone" (Akiflow) in one tap at the end of the day: all unmarked or not-done sessions are proposed to the first free slots, with preview. This is a batch version of the existing per-session "Not done".
- Free/busy flipping and auto-writing outside the app need continuous background sync, which in practice means a server. Cruscotto should stay on-demand and confirm-first.

### Gaps
- I did not find official Reclaim docs on how at-risk status interacts with chunk sizes in 2.0. The 1.0 docs are clearer.
- There is no independent evaluation of energy-based scheduling (Morgen or Structured). Claims come from the vendors.

## 3. What users praise and complain about (over-automation, guilt, notification fatigue)

### Takeaway
Praise goes to **warnings about over-commitment** and **calm, low-pressure design**. The loudest complaint about full auto-schedulers is **constant reshuffling without approval** ("AI calendar anxiety"). Streaks help some learners but **broken streaks demotivate**, and anxious users report guilt from streak notifications. This supports Cruscotto's preview-and-confirm design and suggests forgiving streaks (freezes, rest days) or no streaks at all.

### Cited Findings
- The most cited complaint about Motion in r/productivity threads (per a blog summary): it "reschedules your entire day when one thing changes without letting you approve beforehand". Users describe a schedule that "had changed three times since lunch" and "AI Calendar Anxiety". These are secondhand summaries from competitor blogs, not Reddit reached directly. — [Saner: Motion reviews](https://blog.saner.ai/motion-reviews); [Saner: Sunsama vs Motion](https://blog.saner.ai/sunsama-vs-motion)
- A SaaSworthy user review: "overwhelmed and constantly having to stop and re-plan my tasks and schedule which drove me mad". — [SaaSworthy Motion reviews](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/motion/reviews)
- Users report "random calendar reshuffling that doesn't follow their preferred plan", and reviewers say over-automation makes spontaneous changes hard. — [Morgen: Motion vs Reclaim](https://morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-vs-reclaim)
- Counterpoint: some users find automatic reshuffling "incredibly satisfying when urgent work comes up". — [ClickUp Motion review](https://clickup.com/blog/motion-review/)
- Some users drop Sunsama-style rituals because "the ritual felt like a chore rather than a relief". — [Saner: Sunsama vs Motion](https://blog.saner.ai/sunsama-vs-motion)
- Research summary: broken streaks are demotivating in the short term, and people take costly actions to repair them. — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation)
- A 2026 Northwestern working paper randomized 60,000 students in Peru: streak messages significantly improved math achievement among the 1,500 who took the endline test. — [Northwestern IPR WP-26-05](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf)
- Duolingo says losing a streak can "feel quite demotivating" and that the Streak Freeze gives flexibility for a day off. This is the company's rationale. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- Student anecdotes: a 10 pm "streak at risk" notification "made me panic". Another person deliberately breaks their streak every Shabbat so they don't "feel guilted". In TES, streaks may help reluctant learners but undermine motivated ones. — [Anne Helen Petersen comments](https://annehelen.substack.com/p/how-did-we-get-so-obsessed-with-streaks/comments); [TES](https://tes.com/magazine/article/streaks-motivational-tool-helpful-or-harmful)
- Structured is flagged for feature creep. Shovel is criticised for bugs and price. — [Cool Curation](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk); [App Store Shovel](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1467742357)

### Inferences
- Keep confirm-first, which the CLAUDE.md rule already requires. It is a differentiator against Motion, not a weakness. Show "what changed vs yesterday" in the ricalibro preview to address the "it changed three times" complaint.
- If streaks are added: count weekly target hits instead of daily streaks, allow planned rest days or freezes, and send no late-night "at risk" pushes. Users who don't want streaks need an off switch.
- Use "Not done" and "Skipped" without guilt-heavy wording or red counters. The Sunsama calm-design praise supports this.
- Keep notifications minimal (PWA push without a server is limited anyway).

### Gaps
- I could not read r/GetStudying or r/studytips threads directly. All Reddit sentiment here comes from blog summaries, some of them competitor blogs.
- I found no data on notification fatigue specific to study planners.

## 4. Time tracking and focus timers tied to the plan, and their reception

### Takeaway
Integrated timers (TickTick, Sunsama, Shovel, YPT) log **actual time per task or subject** and compare it with estimates. This is valued for improving estimates and for "honesty" about time. Pomodoro's fixed 25-min blocks are criticised for breaking flow in long study sessions, and Flowtime (stop when you choose, break proportional to work) is a common alternative. A plan-linked timer that records actual minutes against the planned session is fully feasible offline. Cruscotto currently records only Done or Partial %, not minutes.

### Cited Findings
- Sunsama starts time tracking automatically when you begin a task. This "keeps you honest about how long things actually take versus how long you estimated"; its Pomodoro timer is a "small feature". — [Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/schedule-management/sunsama-review/)
- TickTick: per-task pomo estimate vs actual, daily, weekly and monthly focus stats, most productive hours. A focus session can be linked to a task or a habit. — [TickTick blog](https://blog.ticktick.com/2021/01/11/the-pomodoro-technique/); [ClickUp TickTick review](https://clickup.com/learn/topic/task-management/tools/ticktick/)
- YPT: a per-subject study timer with daily totals is the core loop, and it is popular (about 5.6M installs). — [appgoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.pallo.passiontimerscoped); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yeolpumta/id1441909643)
- Flowtime vs Pomodoro: Pomodoro's strict timer "can disrupt the flow state". "25 minutes often isn't enough time to go through a lesson, understand it, and memorize it". Flowtime can fail people who drift without noticing. A 2025 preprint (apparently not peer-reviewed) compared self-regulated breaks, Pomodoro and Flowtime in students and says direct evidence is limited. — [preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202503.0845/v1); [e-student Flowtime guide](https://e-student.org/flowtime-study-method); [Super Productivity blog](https://super-productivity.com/blog/flowtime-technique-pomodoro-alternative/)
- ClickUp's reviewer says students who time-block or use Pomodoro get the most value from TickTick. This is opinion. — [ClickUp TickTick review](https://clickup.com/learn/topic/task-management/tools/ticktick/)

### Inferences
- Add a session timer from the "Oggi" card: start, pause, stop. Offer Pomodoro, Flowtime and plain stopwatch modes. Save actual minutes on the session in Dexie. Prefill "Partial %" from minutes vs planned when the timer stops early. Show "actual vs planned" per subject in the weekly review.
- Feed actual-time ratios back into the deterministic planner, e.g. a per-subject correction factor when the user systematically needs 1.3x the planned time. This is the estimation-learning loop TickTick and Sunsama tout. It is pure local logic and testable.
- Best-time-of-day stats (TickTick "most productive hours") can come from completed-session start times. They could then inform the preferred-window setting in section 2.
- Timers need a screen wake lock and must survive the tab going to background. Use timestamps rather than intervals. This needs no server.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous data on how many students keep using integrated timers long term, or on their effect on planning accuracy.
- Reddit reception of time tracking (guilt from seeing low hours) was not verifiable directly.
