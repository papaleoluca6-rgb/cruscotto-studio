# Learning science → features for Cruscotto Studio

Scope: evidence on effective study techniques and self-regulated learning, mapped to concrete features for a single-user engineering-student study planner (Calendar-based sessions Study → Exercises → Review +1d/+1w/+3w/pre-exam, done/partial/not-done tracking, subject quotas, 7-10 day recalibration, exam-exercise tracker todo / with solution / solved alone, Leitner flashcards, "professor position vs my position").

Evidence-strength legend used below: **STRONG** = multiple meta-analyses with consistent medium+ effects; **MODERATE** = one meta-analysis or consistent lab work with notable heterogeneity/boundary conditions; **WEAK** = single studies, small samples, conflicting results, or only secondary sources.

## Q1. Which study techniques are well supported (retrieval, spacing, interleaving, elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, worked/faded examples), and what app features operationalize them?

### Takeaway
Practice testing (retrieval) and distributed practice (spacing) are the two techniques with the strongest, most general evidence, and spacing *retrieval* is especially effective (g ≈ 0.74). Interleaving, self-explanation and worked examples have medium effects with clear boundary conditions: interleaving needs similar-but-confusable problem types; worked examples help novices and should be faded as skill grows (expertise reversal). Rereading, highlighting and summarizing are low-utility, so the app should push sessions toward testing and problem solving rather than "review = reread".

### Cited Findings
- Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013, *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*) rated 10 techniques. **High utility:** distributed practice and practice testing. **Low utility:** summarization, rereading, highlighting/underlining, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text — [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/?p=82014); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/01/130110111734.htm)
- Elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaved practice received **moderate** utility, partly because less research was available on them; utility was judged on generalization across learning conditions, student characteristics, materials and criterion tasks — [Texas LD Center summary](https://texasldcenter.org/?p=346); [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/?p=82014)
- **Retrieval practice, classroom (STRONG):** Yang, Luo, Vadillo, Yu & Shanks (2021, *Psychological Bulletin* 147(4)) — 222 studies, 48,478 students, 573 effect sizes; testing/quizzing g ≈ 0.50 vs. other strategies (restudy, concept mapping, note-taking). Moderators: control strategy, **format consistency between practice and final test**, corrective feedback, number of test repetitions, timing — [HKBU record](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/testing-quizzing-boosts-classroom-learning-a-systematic-and-meta-/); [Learning and the Brain](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/getting-the-details-just-right-retrieval-practice-2)
- Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017, *Review of Educational Research*), 118 studies: practice tests beat restudy and all other comparison conditions; reported g ≈ 0.70 (vs Rowland 2014 g = 0.50) — 0.70 figure is only from a secondary review; benefit holds at post-secondary level; mixed-format (MC + cued recall) practice tests most effective; testing effect present with or without feedback — [Learning Scientists blog](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/2/9-1); [WSU PDF](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/)
- **Spaced retrieval (STRONG):** Latimier, Peyre & Ramus (2021, *Educational Psychology Review*), 29 studies: spaced vs massed retrieval practice g = 0.74; heterogeneity I² ≈ 49%, "more spacing is not always better" — [x-mol listing](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1340395608147148800); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10950551)
- **Interleaving (MODERATE):** Brunmair & Richter (2019, *Psychological Bulletin* 145(11)): overall g = 0.42, mathematics g = 0.34 (small, on procedural knowledge); I² ≈ 77% (high heterogeneity); effect depends on material — largest when categories are similar/confusable; authors advise evaluating per setting — [Würzburg PDF](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf); [Frontiers correction note](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02296/full)
- **Self-explanation (MODERATE–STRONG):** Bisra et al. (2018, *Educational Psychology Review*): g = 0.55 across 69 effect sizes / 64 reports (random effects); per a secondary summary, all prompt types (interrogative, imperative, pre-directed) helped, pre-directed largest (g = 0.70, k = 8) — [Clearinghouse TUM summary](https://www.clearinghouse.edu.tum.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/CHU-KR-25_ENG_Bisra_2018_Selbsterklaerungen.pdf); [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/self-explanation-powerful-learning-technique-according-meta-analysis-64-studies)
- **Worked examples in math (MODERATE):** Barbieri et al. (2023, *Educational Psychology Review*): g = 0.48 on mathematics performance (RVE); study count reported inconsistently (43 vs 55) across secondary sources; a secondary blog claims adding self-explanation prompts to worked examples did not add benefit (unverified) — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1.pdf); [Learning Scientists guest post](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2024/1/25-1)
- **Faded examples / expertise reversal (MODERATE, mostly lab, well-structured domains):** backward fading (omit last solution step first, then last two, …) supports the transition from studying examples to solving problems (Renkl, Atkinson, Maier & Staley 2002; Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill 2003 fading + prompting); adaptive fading beat fixed fading in tutored problem solving (Salden et al. 2010); expertise reversal: a method that helps novices becomes ineffective for more knowledgeable learners (Kalyuga et al. 2003) — [Wikipedia: worked-example effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect); [Salden et al. 2010, Instructional Science](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11251-009-9107-8); [ASU Kalyuga/Renkl PDF](https://faculty.engineering.asu.edu/mre/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2020/02/Exp_Rev_LI06.pdf)
- Classic worked-example/fading effects were established mainly on well-structured tasks (e.g., mechanics, probability) — [Wikipedia summary citing Nievelstein et al. 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect)

### Inferences (feature mapping)
- **"Review" sessions = retrieval, not rereading.** Make the default content of Review +1d/+1w/+3w a closed-book retrieval task: (a) due flashcards of that topic, (b) 1-3 exam exercises re-solved without solution, or (c) "blank page" free recall of the topic outline, then check. App can prompt at session start "Chiudi gli appunti: cosa ricordi?" and at end ask "Quanto hai ricordato senza guardare? 0-100%". Justified by Dunlosky high-utility + Yang g≈0.50 + Latimier g≈0.74 (STRONG).
- **Exercise tracker already encodes retrieval levels.** The states todo / with solution / solved alone map onto worked example → faded → independent problem solving. Extend with: "re-solved alone after ≥1 week" as a 4th state, and schedule re-attempts of exercises solved with solution (spaced retrieval of procedures). The planner can prioritize exercises "with solution" for re-attempt 2-7 days later (fading by time, MODERATE evidence).
- **Fading suggestion per subject/topic:** if most exercises of a topic are "with solution", the Exercises session suggests "prova i prossimi 2 da solo, guarda la soluzione solo dopo 15 min"; if most are "solved alone", stop suggesting worked examples (expertise reversal). Pure deterministic rule on existing data.
- **Mixed-problem sets (interleaving):** for problem subjects (analysis, physics, circuits), a "Misto" session that pulls exercises from ≥3 already-studied topics of the same subject, shuffled, without topic label shown. Only within a subject and only after each topic has been practiced blocked at least once (interleaving helps discrimination between similar problem types; effect smaller in math, g=0.34, high heterogeneity → MODERATE; make it optional, e.g. in pre-exam phase).
- **"Explain it" prompts (self-explanation / elaborative interrogation):** a short optional text field or checklist at the end of Study sessions: "Perché funziona questo passaggio?", "Come si collega a [argomento precedente]?". Generic prompts work; keep them 1-2 per session to avoid friction. Flashcards could have a "spiega perché" card type. MODERATE evidence (g≈0.55).
- **Flashcards**: Leitner already implements spaced retrieval; add an option for mixed-format cards (recall + recognition) per Adesope; corrective feedback is cheap (show answer) and Yang lists feedback as a moderator.
- **Anti-pattern warning**: if the user marks a Review as "done" but reports method = "riletto/evidenziato", show a gentle tip (low-utility techniques per Dunlosky). Optional method tag on session completion: `retrieval | exercises | reread | summary`.

### Gaps
- Could not verify Adesope 2017's g = 0.70 in the primary paper (secondary review only).
- Did not retrieve a meta-analytic effect size specifically for elaborative interrogation; Dunlosky's "moderate" rating is the best available summary here.
- Barbieri 2023: number of included studies and the "self-explanation adds nothing to worked examples" claim not verified against the paper.
- No meta-analytic effect size found for *faded* vs. non-faded worked examples specifically, nor evidence from university engineering courses with real exams (most fading studies are short lab/tutor studies).

## Q2. Planning fallacy and time-estimate calibration: how do students misjudge study time and what helps?

### Takeaway
Students systematically underestimate completion time — in the classic thesis study, actual time was ~64% longer than the best estimate and most exceeded even their own worst case. Implementation intentions (if-then plans with when/where) reliably help goal attainment, but bias-corrected recent estimates are much smaller (d ≈ 0.15-0.35) than the famous d = 0.65. The app can measure estimate vs. actual directly and auto-inflate plans with a personal correction factor and buffers.

### Cited Findings
- Buehler, Griffin & Ross (1994, *JPSP* 67(3)): honours-thesis students' best estimate averaged 33.9 days vs actual 55.5 days; only ~30% finished by their predicted date; average "worst case" estimate 48.6 days was still exceeded by a majority. Sample: 37 students — [Wikipedia: Planning fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_fallacy); [Buehler et al. 1994 PDF](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/buehler-et-al-1994.pdf); [Silicon Canals commentary](https://siliconcanals.com/t-productivity-advice-usually-starts-after-the-plan-is-made-but-decades-of-research-suggest-many-missed-deadlines-begin-earlier-with-an-optimistic-estimate-of-how-long-the-work-will-take/)
- Implementation intentions: Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), 94 independent tests, d = 0.65 on goal attainment, mostly vs. a goal-intention control — [Konstanz record](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes); [Stafforini](https://stafforini.com/works/gollwitzer-2006-implementation-intentions-and/)
- A 2024 synthesis (Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer, 642 tests) reported publication-bias-corrected estimates of d = 0.35 (trim-and-fill) and d = 0.15 (robust Bayesian, 95% CI 0.08-0.22) — only via secondary source — [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/t-implementation-intentions-when-where-how-goal-achievement/)
- Monitoring goal progress: Harkin et al. (2016, *Psychological Bulletin* 142(2)), 138 RCTs, N = 19,951: monitoring promotes attainment; effects larger when progress is **physically recorded** and when **reported/made public**. Secondary source gives d = 0.40 on attainment (d = 1.98 on monitoring frequency); reported private monitoring d = 0.47 vs unreported 0.19; most included studies were health-related — [White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/1/bul%20harkin%20raw%20final.pdf); [Science for Work summary](https://scienceforwork.com/blog/goal-monitoring/)

### Inferences (feature mapping)
- **Estimated vs actual minutes per session type and subject.** Each Calendar event has a planned duration (estimate); on marking done/partial, ask for actual time (or infer: partial = planned × reported %). Store ratio actual/planned per (subject, session type). Show a "fattore di realismo" e.g. "Le tue sessioni di Esercizi di Fisica durano 1.4× il previsto".
- **Auto-correction in the recalibration:** the deterministic planner multiplies quota-hours by the personal ratio (rolling median of last N sessions, clamped e.g. 1.0-2.0) before filling the 7-10 days. This is reference-class forecasting from the user's own history — directly targets the planning fallacy (evidence for the bias is STRONG/classic; evidence that this specific correction improves outcomes is an inference, not tested here).
- **Buffers:** reserve a fixed share of free slots per week (e.g., 15-20%) as unplanned "recupero" so that moved/partial sessions have somewhere to land without cascading. Buehler data (most exceeded even worst case) argue for buffers rather than just better point estimates.
- **"Professor vs me" gap as planning-fallacy check:** projected date at which "where I am" catches "where the professor is" given actual recent throughput (topics/week), compared with exam date → early warning when the projection lands after the exam.
- **Implementation intentions:** the app's Calendar events already specify when; add an optional "dove / primo passo" field ("Biblioteca, apro esercizio 3 del 2023") and an if-then fallback ("Se salto la sessione, la recupero nel primo buco libero dopo cena"). Keep it optional and lightweight — effect real but probably small (d ≈ 0.15-0.35 bias-corrected).
- **Progress monitoring = recorded:** done/partial/not-done is exactly the "physically recorded" monitoring that Harkin found more effective; a weekly summary screen strengthens it (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis found specifically on interventions that *reduce* the planning fallacy in students (e.g., unpacking tasks, reference-class prompts); the app's correction-factor approach is an inference.
- Bias-corrected implementation-intention estimates (2024) only seen via a secondary source.

## Q3. Metacognition and self-assessment: are confidence ratings, judgments of learning (JOLs) and exam-readiness estimates accurate and useful?

### Takeaway
Self-judgments are only moderately accurate: immediate judgments right after studying are inflated by fluency, delayed judgments (made later, from a cue only) are much more accurate, and low performers overpredict exam scores persistently even with practice-test feedback. So the app should base "readiness" mainly on objective retrieval data (flashcard recall, exercises solved alone, timed mock score) and use self-ratings only as delayed, cue-based inputs compared against performance.

### Cited Findings
- Rhodes & Tauber (2011, *Psychological Bulletin* 137(1)), meta-analysis of the delayed-JOL effect (≈98 effect sizes): delayed JOLs are more accurate than immediate JOLs; cue-only delayed JOLs most accurate, because they rely on retrieval success rather than short-term memory fluency — [HED task description](https://www.hedtags.org/hed-task/tasks/hedtsk_judgment_of_learning.html); [Springer article citing it](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13421-020-01025-5.pdf)
- Accuracy measures: **resolution** (gamma correlation between judgments and recall — which items I know) vs **calibration** (mean judgment vs mean recall — over/underconfidence) — [HED task description](https://www.hedtags.org/hed-task/tasks/hedtsk_judgment_of_learning.html)
- Hacker, Bol, Horgan & Rakow (2000, *J. Educational Psychology* 92): across three semester exams, low performers remained overconfident, even when the class emphasized accurate self-evaluation — [Learning Scientists](https://learningscientists.org/blog/2017/2/16-1)
- Low-performing students confidently overpredict grade performance throughout the semester; miscalibration and confidence stable across tests (2023, *Journal of Intelligence*) — [PMC10607382](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10607382/)
- Osterhage (2021, intro biology): voluntary practice tests associated with better performance and better calibration overall, but many lowest performers still overestimated despite practice-test feedback; high performers tended to become underconfident — [PMC8442020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8442020/)

### Inferences (feature mapping)
- **Readiness score per subject = mostly objective.** Combine: % of exam exercises "solved alone" (weighted by recency), flashcard recall rate at last review, last mock-exam score, and topic coverage ("where I am" vs syllabus). Show the self-rating separately, not blended invisibly.
- **Delayed, cue-only confidence ratings:** don't ask "how well do you know this?" at the end of the Study session (inflated). Ask it at the start of the next Review (+1d) *before* retrieving: show only the topic name → "Quanto pensi di ricordare? 0-100" → then do retrieval → record actual. This is literally the delayed cue-only JOL paradigm.
- **Calibration chart:** plot predicted vs actual (per topic or per mock exam). Show "sovrastimi in media di X punti" — gives the user a personal correction. Evidence that feedback alone fixes overconfidence is weak (Hacker; Osterhage), so pair it with behavioral consequences: planner adds extra review slots for topics where predicted − actual > threshold.
- **Exam prediction before each mock/real exam** (predicted score, then enter actual) — cheap to collect, builds a per-user calibration history; useful mostly for the recalibrator, not as motivation.
- **Use JOL resolution for flashcards:** "Facile/Difficile" self-ratings in Leitner are fine for item ordering (resolution), but the box progression should depend on correct/incorrect recall, not on self-rated ease.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve the effect-size magnitude of the delayed-JOL effect from Rhodes & Tauber itself (only that delayed > immediate).
- No direct evidence found that showing calibration graphs in an app improves exam outcomes; feature value is inferred.

## Q4. Exam preparation: optimal spacing relative to exam date, mock exams under timed conditions, past papers.

### Takeaway
The optimal gap between study and review grows with the time until the test but shrinks as a proportion of it: ~20-40% of the retention interval for a 1-week horizon, ~5-10% for a 1-year horizon (Cepeda et al. 2008, >1,350 learners). For a typical semester exam 3-10 weeks away, gaps of roughly 1-2 weeks between reviews are in the sweet spot; overly short gaps hurt more than slightly long ones. Practice tests whose format matches the final exam give larger gains, supporting timed mocks on past papers.

### Cited Findings
- Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted & Pashler (2008, *Psychological Science* 19(11)): >1,350 participants, review gaps up to 3.5 months, final test up to 1 year later. Increasing the gap first improved then gradually reduced final performance; optimal gap increased with test delay; as a proportion of test delay it fell from ~20-40% (1-week test) to ~5-10% (1-year test) — [USF Digital Commons abstract](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1766/); [PDF](https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaVulRohrerWixtedPashler-PS-2008.pdf)
- Examples from secondary coverage: 1-day gap optimal for 7-day retention; 21-day gap optimal for 350-day retention — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/11/081118141708.htm)
- Spaced retrieval beats massed retrieval (g = 0.74), but more spacing is not always better — [x-mol](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1340395608147148800); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10950551)
- Classroom testing effect is moderated by **test-format consistency** and material matching between practice and final test; transfer-appropriate processing is one of the supported explanatory theories — [HKBU record (Yang et al. 2021)](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/testing-quizzing-boosts-classroom-learning-a-systematic-and-meta-/); [Learning and the Brain](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/getting-the-details-just-right-retrieval-practice-2)
- Practice tests improve calibration overall but not reliably for the lowest performers — [PMC8442020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8442020/)

### Inferences (feature mapping)
- **Exam-relative review scheduler:** current fixed +1d/+1w/+3w/pre-exam is consistent with Cepeda for a ~1-3 month horizon. Improve by making the last gaps depend on days-to-exam: if the exam is <3 weeks away, compress (+1d, +4d, +10d, pre-exam); if >2 months, keep +3w and add +6w. Rule of thumb: next gap ≈ 10-20% of remaining days to exam, min 1 day (inference from 20-40% @1wk and 5-10% @1yr).
- **Never collapse everything into the last days:** the recalibrator should warn when >X% of a subject's planned review minutes fall in the final 3 days before the exam (massing).
- **Mock exam sessions:** new session type "Simulazione" = a past paper (from the exam-exercise tracker) under the real time limit, closed book, scored afterwards. Schedule 2-3 per subject in the final 2-3 weeks, spaced (not on consecutive days). Record score, time used, predicted score (calibration, Q3). Format-matching is supported by Yang's moderator (MODERATE inference: no meta-analysis on timed vs untimed mocks found).
- **Past-paper coverage metric:** % of past-exam exercise types solved alone at least once and again after ≥1 week → feeds the readiness score and the quota's "backlog" term.
- **Pre-exam slot:** final review the day(s) before should be retrieval (flashcards, re-solve exercises previously failed), not new material.

### Gaps
- No meta-analytic evidence found on timed vs untimed practice exams or on optimal number of mock exams; recommendation rests on format-consistency moderator and transfer-appropriate processing.
- Cepeda's materials were facts/trivia; transfer of the gap/retention ratios to multi-step engineering problem solving is untested in the sources found.
- Cepeda's exact per-condition optimal gaps for 35- and 70-day delays not retrieved.

## Q5. Motivation and adherence: streaks, weekly review/reflection, goal setting, avoiding guilt/overload; breaks, Pomodoro, sleep and scheduling.

### Takeaway
Recording and reviewing progress has solid support (Harkin 2016); streaks motivate while intact but broken streaks demotivate, an effect softened when the streak can be "repaired" — so avoid hard streak counters or make them forgiving. Short breaks give small boosts to vigor/fatigue (d ≈ 0.35) but not to performance on demanding tasks; fixed Pomodoro-style breaks have mixed evidence vs self-chosen breaks. Sleep duration predicts GPA (−0.07 GPA per lost hour; harm below ~6 h), so the planner should never schedule study into sleep time.

### Cited Findings
- Progress monitoring (Harkin et al. 2016): 138 RCTs, monitoring promotes goal attainment; larger effects when progress is physically recorded and when reported/made public — [White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/1/bul%20harkin%20raw%20final.pdf); [APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2015/10/progress-goals?hl=pt-BR)
- Streaks: Silverman & Barasch (2023, *Journal of Consumer Research* 49(6)) — intact streaks motivate continuation; broken streaks reduce subsequent engagement; effect diminished when participants could **repair** the streak and amplified when they felt personally responsible for breaking it; streak defined as ≥3 consecutive repetitions — [Psychology Today (Markman)](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation); [authors' write-up](https://mot-backup.campaignme.com/keeping-consumer-streaks-alive-by-alixandra-barasch-and-jackie-silverman); contradicting nuance: Decision Lab claims repairable streaks reduce motivation to keep them — [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification)
- Habit formation (Lally et al., UCL): missing a single opportunity did not significantly affect habit formation (secondary source) — [Yu-kai Chou blog](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/streak-design-gamification-motivation-burnout/)
- Micro-breaks: Albulescu et al. (2022, *PLOS ONE*), 22 samples, N = 2,335: vigor d = 0.36, fatigue d = 0.35 (both significant, small); overall performance d = 0.16 (n.s.), significant only for low-cognitive-demand tasks; longer breaks → bigger performance effect; highly depleting tasks may need >10-min breaks — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/c6f37b6ba159469296b2cb968b65505f); [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/taking-a-quick-break-at-work-is-good-for-you)
- Pomodoro vs self-regulated breaks: Biwer et al. (2023, *British J. Educational Psychology*), 87 Dutch university students, 1 day: systematic breaks (24/6 and 12/3) → better mood and apparent efficiency (similar task completion in less time); self-regulated group studied and paused longer and reported more fatigue/distraction. A 2025 follow-up reported faster fatigue increase with Pomodoro breaks and no overall difference in task outcomes → WEAK/mixed — [Maastricht record](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/understanding-effort-regulation-comparing-pomodoro-breaks-and-sel); [PMC12292963](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/); [MDPI Behavioral Sciences 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/15/7/861)
- Sleep: Creswell et al. (2023, *PNAS*), >600 first-year students, 5 samples, 3 universities, Fitbit-measured: average 6.5 h/night; each hour of sleep lost → −0.07 end-of-term GPA; below ~6 h sleep becomes harmful; early-term sleep predicted GPA 5-9 weeks later — [CMU PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/behavioral-health-lab/Papers%20and%20CV/Creswell%20et%20al.,%202023%2054.pdf); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2023-02-nightly-key-student-success.html)

### Inferences (feature mapping)
- **Weekly review screen ("Riepilogo settimana")**: planned vs done minutes per subject, % done/partial/not-done, estimate-vs-actual ratio, readiness changes, professor-vs-me gap; ends with 2 prompts: "Cosa ha funzionato?" / "Cosa cambio la prossima settimana?" and then triggers the 7-10 day recalibration. Physically recorded monitoring is supported (Harkin); reflection prompts themselves are an inference.
- **No punitive streaks.** If any streak exists, make it weekly ("settimane con ≥80% delle sessioni chiave fatte") rather than daily, allow repair (a recovered session counts), and never show "streak perso" in red. Prefer cumulative counters (total retrieval sessions, exercises solved alone) which cannot "break". Supported by Silverman & Barasch + Lally (MODERATE/WEAK).
- **Overload guard:** recalibration caps daily study minutes (user setting) and, when backlog exceeds capacity, shows an explicit triage ("non ci sta tutto: queste 3 sessioni a priorità più bassa vengono tolte") instead of silently cramming — ties to planning-fallacy buffers (Q2) and avoids the guilt spiral of a perpetually red backlog (inference; no direct study found).
- **Not-done ≠ failure framing:** marking "not done" offers immediate "sposta nel primo spazio libero" — this is the "repair" that softens broken-streak demotivation.
- **Session length & breaks:** default session blocks of 50-90 min with an optional built-in break timer (e.g., 25/5 or 50/10, user choice); do not hard-code Pomodoro — evidence is mixed. For heavy problem-solving sessions suggest longer breaks (>10 min) between blocks (Albulescu).
- **Sleep-protecting scheduler:** settings for "sonno" window (default e.g. 23:30-07:30 in Asia/Shanghai); the planner never places sessions there and warns if the user manually moves a session into it or if the pre-exam plan implies late nights. Optional sleep-hours log in the weekly review. STRONG-ish correlational evidence (Creswell, observational, not causal).
- **Goal setting:** per-subject weekly goal expressed as concrete outputs (e.g., "5 esercizi d'esame risolti da solo", "1 simulazione") rather than hours — aligns monitoring with retrieval outcomes.

### Gaps
- Silverman & Barasch effect sizes not retrieved; repair-mechanic findings conflict between sources.
- Lally et al. habit-formation finding only via a non-peer-reviewed blog.
- No peer-reviewed meta-analysis on Pomodoro specifically was found; only Biwer 2023 and a 2025 follow-up.
- Creswell 2023 is about sleep duration; no finding on sleep regularity retrieved (a separate 2019 study reportedly addressed consistency but was not checked).
- No direct evidence found on guilt/overload effects of planner apps or on self-compassion interventions for procrastination (not searched within the tool-call budget).
